**Cue count: 31.** Comedic register, ~7 min — that's roughly one visual beat every 13 seconds, which is right for this format.

## Must be created

No search will produce these — they need making (After Effects, or a motion designer):

- **Cue 22** — minibar shelf cutaway with load cell firing. The most important graphic in the video; it explains the mechanism the whole back half depends on.
- **Cue 23** — infrared beam grid animation (also the legally safe replacement for heist-movie footage).
- **Cue 9** — price-breakdown stack ($1 → $10).
- **Cues 8, 12, 21, 27** — stat cards. Build one template, reuse four times.
- **Cues 3, 16, 17** — pure on-screen text beats.

## Must be shot

Shoot-day dependencies. Most need access to an actual hotel room with a minibar:

- **Cues 1, 4, 14, 31** — the same minibar, three lighting states (neutral, late-night, closed/empty). Shoot together in one session; the visual rhyme across the video depends on it being the *same* unit.
- **Cue 7** — the vodka-refill re-enactment. Highest-value shot in the video.
- **Cue 15** — elevator doors/floor numbers.
- **Cue 24** — hand lifting and replacing an item + phone charge notification.
- **Cue 6** — talking-head, no b-roll.

## Low-confidence cues

Flagging before anyone spends time hunting:

- **Cue 18 (Madison Hotel 1963)** — the hotel is real and well documented, but period-specific interior shots showing an *early minibar unit* may not exist in any accessible archive. Likely fallback: exterior only.
- **Cue 19 (Hong Kong Hilton)** — exterior archival almost certainly exists (it was a landmark, demolished 1995), but expect rights-managed rather than public domain. Hong Kong government/press archives are the best bet.
- **Cue 11 (staff restocking minibars)** — specific enough that generic stock may not cover it; may need to be shot instead.
- **Robert Arnold** (the F&B director in the origin story) — I doubt a usable portrait exists. Not listed as a cue for that reason; if one turns up it'd be a nice addition to cue 19.
