The only real rule: a hotel can't run its *own* separate giveaway alongside the survey to juice turnout. **The scale, which makes the "it's just a popularity contest with a PDF" read land, not just a cynical aside**: Travel + Leisure's own figures put the 2024 survey at 186,000+ readers casting 700,000+ votes across 8,700+ properties, policed for fraud by a named research partner, Proof Insights ([2024 FAQ, via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/t-l-worlds-best-awards-094304452.html)) — this is a real, large, genuinely-run survey, not a sham, which is what makes the "and also, here's the official PDF on how to lobby your own guests to win it" detail land as funny rather than as an accusation of fraud. **Why Capella specifically, beyond "it's a named brand"**: this creator can draw a direct, three-way contrast most travel content never bothers to make — Capella's World's 50 Best property placements (Capella Bangkok at No. 2, voted by ~800 anonymous industry experts) sit in the same season as Capella's Travel + Leisure brand title (voted by 200,000+ readers, campaignable with room-drop cards) — two completely different definitions of "best," both real, both currently true of the same company, and nobody puts them side by side. **Honest limitations, stated rather than papered over**: the exact 2026 announcement date for Capella's fourth-year win isn't confirmed in this round's sourcing — one source implies July 2026 by pattern-matching the prior year's release timing, but no source gives the 2026 date directly, so `story-researcher` should pull the actual 2026 PR Newswire or Travel+Leisure release before stating a date on camera. No source found this round states whether Capella itself specifically ran a room-drop or email campaign for its own win — the piece should present the *mechanism Travel + Leisure makes available to all candidates*, not claim Capella individually used it, unless that's separately confirmed.
