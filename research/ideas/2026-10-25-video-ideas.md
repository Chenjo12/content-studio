An Otis Elevator Company estimate, repeated across multiple outlets but tracing to a single underlying figure rather than independent confirmations, puts this at roughly **85% of elevator panels** omitting the number — i.e. the overwhelming default in the industry is theater, not architecture. **The payoff beat — what happens when a hotel owns it instead**: Baltimore's **Belvedere Hotel** didn't hide its 13th floor, it named a restaurant after it — "The 13th Floor" — turning the supposedly cursed number into the building's actual landmark; **Sheraton on the Falls** did the same, converting its 13th floor into a restaurant rather than erasing it from the panel. A secondhand but illustrative anecdote (unverified, single-source, flagged accordingly) describes the **Anatole in Dallas** as having no 13th-floor elevator button at all — despite the floor obviously existing if you count the building from outside. **The hospitality-insider framing that elevates this past a generic "fun fact" listicle** (this exact topic has been run by Scripps/Gallup-poll-citing local-news affiliates and travel blogs dozens of times, per today's search results — the differentiator has to be the editorial read, not the trivia): this is purely a guest-psychology and signage decision made by hotel operators and developers, not a structural one — the same insider-mechanism move as relabeling a line item to avoid friction, applied to an entire floor. **Honest limitation, stated rather than papered over**: the "85%" Otis figure is repeated by Culture Trip, Scripps/local-news affiliates, and others, but all trace to the same original attribution rather than independent data — `story-researcher` should frame it as "widely cited, single-sourced" rather than verified. No named-collaboration-brand property was found doing either the hiding or the owning-it move — `story-researcher` should check whether any of Bvlgari/Four Seasons/Capella/W/Wynn Palace's own towers have documented 13th-floor numbering (their own floor plans or press may confirm this one way or the other and would meaningfully strengthen the asset fit).
