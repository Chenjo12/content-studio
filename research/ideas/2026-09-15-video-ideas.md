## Ideas

1. **"A British Governor Signed Hong Kong Away in Room 336 — and the Hotel Just Kept Running the Front Desk."** On December 25, 1941 ("Black Christmas" in Hong Kong history), after 18 days of fighting, Governor Sir Mark Young signed the Instrument of Surrender inside **The Peninsula Hong Kong** — specifically in Room 336 on the third floor — marking the first time a British colony had ever surrendered to an invading force. Young was then held under house arrest in the hotel for two months before being shipped to a POW camp in Shanghai. The Japanese Governor of Hong Kong, Lieutenant General Rensuke Isogai, subsequently used the hotel as his personal residence and the occupation's administrative headquarters from February to April 1942, before the hotel reopened for business that same April — under a new name, **"The Toa Hotel"** (East Asia Hotel) — and operated under that name for the remainder of the nearly four-year occupation, before reverting to "The Peninsula" at the war's end ([Localiiz](https://www.localiiz.com/post/culture-history-peninsula-hotel-hong-kong), [Hospitality On](https://hospitality-on.com/en/hospitality/peninsula-hong-kong-white-elephant-became-grand-old-lady-far-east), [Wikipedia — Japanese occupation of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_Hong_Kong), [J3 Consultants HK](https://www.j3consultantshongkong.com/j3c-blog/peninsula-hotel-hong-kong-complete-history-insider-guide)). The mechanism that makes this more than a plaque-reading recap: a hotel is a building that's contractually obligated to keep functioning as a hotel — checking guests in, running a kitchen, doing laundry — even while the government inside it changes by force, and even while it's stripped of its own name. That operational continuity through occupation, rename, and reopening is a specifically hospitality-industry way of reading what "the hotel survived the war" actually means, distinct from a standard wartime-history retelling. This is a genuinely new hotel-history subject for this project — never proposed in any prior batch — and a still-operating, globally famous property, not an obscure ruin. Source: today's pulse-check (not tied to today's calendar date — the December 25 anniversary is three months out, so this is evergreen research rather than a dated news hook, flagged honestly). Platform(s): YouTube (video essay — needs the room). Asset fit: no named-collaboration-brand tie — an honest editorial-coherence stretch, same category as the Ritz Paris, Manila Hotel, and Gulf Hotel Houston entries already in the queue — but the Peninsula's continued global prominence and the property's specific relevance to a Hong Kong-literate, Chinese-speaking audience give it real standing beyond a generic history pick. Register: **history — gripping register.** This involves a real colonial government, real named historical figures (Sir Mark Young, Lt. Gen. Rensuke Isogai), and a nearly four-year occupation with real civilian consequences beyond the hotel itself — needs the same extra-sourcing-rigor pass CLAUDE.md already holds the Ritz Paris and Manila Hotel entries to before any scripting, including verifying the Room 336 detail and occupation dates against a primary or academic source, not just travel-history blogs. No comedic treatment — this is not Rise-of-X material. Hook: the room number is still on the door, and the hotel will tell you what happened there — the surrender of an entire British colony, signed on a desk that was, an hour earlier, just furniture.
