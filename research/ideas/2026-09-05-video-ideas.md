1. **"IHG Just Renamed 14 Kyoto Hotels Overnight — Here's What You're Actually Paying For When You Book By Brand."** On August 24, 2026, IHG signed a deal with Singapore-based operator GCP Hospitality to convert 14 existing Kyoto hotels (currently operating under various "M's" names, 1,063 rooms total) into IHG's Garner and Holiday Inn Express brands over the next 12 months ([Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4134058/ihg-hotels-resorts-signs-landmark-14-hotel-portfolio-deal-in-japan), [IHG press release](https://www.ihgplc.com/en/news-and-media/news-releases/2026/ihg-signs-landmark-14-hotel-portfolio-deal-in-japan), [LoyaltyLobby](https://loyaltylobby.com/2026/08/24/ihg-signs-a-deal-to-convert-14-kyoto-ms-hotels-under-garner-holiday-inn-express-brands/)). The buildings are already open and operating — this is a pure conversion deal, not new construction. The mechanism worth actually explaining: a hotel brand conversion doesn't usually mean new ownership, new staff, or even a new building — it means the property buys into a reservation/distribution system, a loyalty program's member base, and a quality-standard audit process, in exchange for a franchise fee and a sign change. Guests will start earning IHG One Rewards points at addresses that, physically, may barely change. Source: today's pulse-check. Platform(s): XHS (insider short cut), Bilibili (knowledge-format fit, same lane as the Forbes-inspector piece already in the queue), YouTube (video essay). Asset fit: strong — this is real hospitality-operations mechanism knowledge (what a franchise conversion transaction actually is) rather than travel description, and it's a concrete, timely instance of a mechanism the creator has flagged interest in before (brand-vs-product value, in `production-queue.csv`) without repeating either of those queued ideas — this is its own case study, not a re-suggestion. Register: comedic-dry insider. Hook: you'll soon earn hotel points at 14 Kyoto properties that didn't change owners, staff, or buildings — they changed one thing, the sign out front. What did IHG actually just buy, and what did GCP Hospitality actually just sell?

2. **"The Man Who Invented the Boutique Hotel Just Opened One on the Site of the Hotel That Beat Him to the Punch."** Ian Schrager — who, with Steve Rubell, ran Studio 54, went to prison for tax fraud, then came out and opened Morgans in New York in 1984, widely credited as the first true boutique hotel — is opening PUBLIC West Hollywood on July 15, 2026: a 137-room property on the Sunset Strip, designed with architect John Pawson, built on the literal site of the former Standard Hollywood ([WEHOonline](https://wehoonline.com/146303-2/), [Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/announcement/41013530.html), [Hospitality Design](https://hospitalitydesign.com/news/public-west-hollywood-california-ian-schrager/185295)). The Standard was André Balazs's own landmark lifestyle-hotel brand — a rival, same-era answer to the category Schrager started. This is a real, well-sourced story about the actual invention of an entire hotel category (the "boutique hotel" didn't always exist — it's a specific 1980s response to bland corporate hotel chains, and this creator's Swiss-hospitality-management training is exactly the credential that can explain *why* it was a structural break, not just a design one), told through a genuinely strange real-world coincidence: two eras of the same insurgent hotel category, on the same physical corner. Source: today's pulse-check. Platform(s): YouTube (Speeed's own "Stüssy: the rise of America's 2nd most important clothing brand" mock-serious brand-history format is the direct template here — see `research/channels/speeed.md`), XHS (shorter insider cut). Asset fit: strong — draws directly on hospitality-design history and editorial credibility, not generic travel commentary. Register: comedic-dry (Speeed's mock-serious brand-history register, applied to a hotel category instead of an object). Hook: one nightclub promoter got banned from his own club, went to prison, came out and invented a category of hotel that's now worth billions — and just opened his newest one on the exact corner where his old rival's competing version used to stand.

3. **"What It Actually Takes to Reopen a Resort After a Hurricane: Royalton Blue Waters, 1,200 Jobs, and 10 Months."** Hurricane Melissa forced Royalton Hotels & Resorts to close properties across Jamaica; Royalton Blue Waters and Royalton Hideaway Blue Waters (578 rooms combined) are scheduled to reopen September 15, 2026, restoring roughly 1,200 staff positions, after the company delayed its original timeline to prioritize a "safety-first" standard over speed ([PAX News](https://www.paxnews.com/news/hotel/royalton-adjusts-jamaica-resort-reopening-dates-following-hurricane-melissa), [Royalton press release](https://www.royaltonresorts.com/press/royalton-hotels--resorts-outlines-comprehensive-support-plan-for-jamaicas-recovery), [Hotel Online](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/royalton-negril-reopens-as-jamaicas-tourism-recovery-increases-momentum)). The angle worth telling isn't "resort reopens" as a press-release beat — it's the actual sequence a hospitality-management-trained eye can explain that a general news outlet won't: why a physically repairable building still can't reopen for months (structural re-certification, insurance claims processing, water/power/food-safety systems, re-hiring and re-training staff at scale, tour-operator and travel-agent booking systems needing to re-list the property) — the real operational mechanics behind "we reopen when it's safe," not just the sentiment. Source: today's pulse-check. Platform(s): YouTube (video essay), Bilibili. Asset fit: moderate-strong — no named collaboration-brand tie, but the reopening-mechanics explanation is genuine hospitality-operations insider knowledge. Register: gripping-dramatic, but note — this is a recovery/resilience story, not a tragedy piece; there's no fatality framing here, so it doesn't require the same extra-sourcing-rigor bar CLAUDE.md sets for the Ritz Paris/Manila Hotel entries already in the queue, but real livelihoods are involved and it still needs care, sourcing discipline, and no sensationalizing. Hook: Hurricane Melissa didn't just damage two Jamaican resorts, it closed 1,200 people's jobs — and reopening on September 15 was never going to be as simple as sweeping up.
