1. **"There's a Fire Code in Los Angeles With a Hotel's Name On It — Because of What Happened Here Exactly 56 Years Ago Today."** In the pre-dawn hours of September 13, 1970, an arson fire tore through the Ponet Square Hotel and Apartments at 1249 S. Grand Ave in Los Angeles — a four-story, 86-unit building dating to around 1910 — killing 19 people and injuring 50+. The mechanism that makes this more than a tragedy-of-the-day retelling: fire inspectors had condemned the building back in **1941** specifically over its open, unenclosed stairwell — the exact design flaw that let the fire race from the lobby to every upper floor in minutes — and that cited, known hazard sat uncorrected for **29 years** until it killed people. The direct regulatory consequence gives the piece its hook: Los Angeles passed what's still informally called the **"Ponet fire doors ordinance"** — a rule requiring any building of two or more floors to have enclosed stairways with heavy doors capable of containing a fire for at least an hour — a piece of the modern fire code that is, quite literally, named after this building. This is the "how did we get from that building to today's fire code" mechanism a hospitality-operations background can genuinely explain (why enclosed stairwells and fire-rated doors are non-negotiable in any hotel built or renovated since), applied to a dead-precise anniversary rather than a round-number one. — Source: today's pulse-check ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponet_Square_Hotel_and_Apartments_fire), [LAFD History](https://www.lafire.com/famous_fires/1970-0913_PonetHotelFire/091370_PonetHotelFire.htm), [LAFRA](https://www.lafra.org/lafd-history-ponet-square-hotel-fire-1970/)). Platform(s): YouTube (video essay — needs the room a short can't give). Asset fit: no named-collaboration-brand tie — an honest editorial-coherence stretch, same category as the Ritz Paris, Manila Hotel, Gulf Hotel Houston, and Manor Hotel Quezon City entries already in the queue — but real standing to explain *why* this specific architectural rule exists and is still load-bearing in every hotel built today. Register: **history — gripping register.** Real named deaths (arson-caused, not accidental), so this needs the same extra-sourcing-rigor pass CLAUDE.md already holds the Ritz Paris and Manila Hotel entries to before any scripting — no sensationalizing the "known hazard ignored for 29 years" detail beyond what the sources support. Hook: a fire code with a building's name stamped on it, and the twenty-nine-year paper trail proving everyone already knew what would happen.
