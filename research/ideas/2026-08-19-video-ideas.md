2. **"The Hotel Where the Burglar Bars Became the Coffin."** Twenty-five years ago yesterday (August 18, 2001), a fire broke out on the third floor of the Manor Hotel in Quezon City, Philippines — an electrical short near a stockroom beside a karaoke bar and restaurant, which spread fast through flammable furnishings, pushed by wind. **74 people died**, making it the second-worst structural fire in Philippine history. The reason the death toll was so high isn't really about the fire itself — it's an institutional-failure story: guest rooms had iron burglar bars bolted over the windows (installed to deter theft) with no fire escapes, no alarms, and corridors that were locked or obstructed, so trapped guests had nowhere to go. The hotel had been operating without a business permit and was already flagged for closure in 1999 over National Fire Code and National Building Code violations — and kept running anyway. In 2019, two former Quezon City officials (a city engineer and the electrical division chief) were convicted of graft and sentenced to 30 years; the hotel's owners, William and Rebecca Genato, were also sentenced. This is a genuine hospitality-safety-systems story a real hotel-management background can speak to with authority most true-crime-style coverage can't — not just "here's what happened" but "here's exactly which modern fire-code and inspection standards exist *because* stories like this kept happening." — Source: today's pulse-check ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manor_Hotel_fire), [Rappler](https://www.rappler.com/philippines/229310-ex-quezon-city-officials-manor-hotel-owners-appeal-conviction-hotel-fire-2001/), [GMA News](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/metro/689926/ex-qc-officials-convicted-over-2001-manor-hotel-fire/story/), [Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/nation/2019/04/01/1906179/ex-quezon-city-officials-get-30-years-2001-hotel-fire)). Platform(s): YouTube (video-essay format fits the gravity here). Asset fit: strong, but distinct from the prior history entries — this is a regulatory/safety-systems failure rather than wartime history (Ritz Paris, Manila Hotel) or a single structural defect (Hyatt Regency KC), so a Swiss-hotel-management background genuinely earns a different kind of authority to explain it: what the fire-code and inspection regime looks like *today*, specifically because of failures like this one. Register: **history — gripping register.** Needs a full story-researcher pass with the same extra sourcing rigor the Ritz Paris and Manila Hotel briefs require — real named victims and a real criminal conviction record, so nothing here should be treated as scripting-ready without independent verification.
