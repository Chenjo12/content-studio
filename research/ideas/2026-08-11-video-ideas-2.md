## Ideas

1. **"The Rise of the Do Not Disturb Sign" — a mock-serious deep dive on the most ignored object in a hotel room.** One collector has amassed over 15,000 different Do Not Disturb signs from hotels worldwide — treat that fact as the cold open, then trace the sign's murky, undocumented origin (probably early-1900s prestige hotels, no one actually knows for sure) through its 1970s mass-tourism multilingual redesign to today's electronic replacements. Same mechanism as "The Rise of the Minibar": total sincerity applied to something nobody thinks twice about. — Source: today's pulse-check ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/travel/article/hotel-do-not-disturb-signs-collection), [Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/do-not-disturb-signs-collection)). Platform(s): YouTube. Asset fit: strong — hospitality-industry framing explains *why* hotels standardized on this object, which a non-insider retelling can't do. Register: comedic. Hook: "someone has 15,000 of these and I have questions."

2. **"The Key That Got Smarter Than the Guest" — hotel key evolution from brass to RFID to your phone.** Brass keys as status objects → 1970s-80s magnetic stripe (vulnerable to demagnetizing next to your own phone, the source of a universal guest complaint) → 1990s smart chips → 2000s RFID tap-to-enter → now literally your phone. Frame as a mini-history of hotels quietly trading elegance for friction-reduction, one generation at a time. — Source: today's pulse-check ([PLI Cards](https://www.plicards.com/card-talk/evolution-of-hotel-key-cards), [RFIDcard.com](https://www.rfidcard.com/the-history-of-hotel-cards/)). Platform(s): YouTube. Asset fit: strong — same "Rise of X" mechanism, second application, proving it's a repeatable format not a one-off. Register: comedic. Hook: the demagnetized-key-next-to-your-phone complaint everyone's had, then zooming out to the century-long story behind it.

3. **Grading a real stay against my own rubric, Walk With Me Tim-style, but insider.** Adopt the room/food/service/value structure that's proven and growing in the hotel-review lane (Walk With Me Tim, ~950K-994K subs) but layer in what Swiss hotel-management training actually notices at each stage — not just "the room was nice" but *why* the layout, staffing ratio, or service sequencing is or isn't working. Anchor it in a real property from the creator's actual collaboration history (Capella, W, Bvlgari, Four Seasons, Wynn Palace). — Source: trend-scout angle #1. Platform(s): YouTube, XHS. Asset fit: very strong — this is the single highest-confidence angle from today's trend-scout review. Register: comedic, with genuine substance underneath. Hook: using the exact structure a proven format already uses, then visibly breaking from it the moment industry knowledge kicks in.

4. **"I'm Not Going to Pretend This Was Perfect" — an explicit anti-influencer positioning piece.** Named directly after Speeed's "anti-hype" stance (modest, completed, unsensational content over corporate-sponsored gloss) — a video that states outright the difference between a paid-stay glowing review and an actually critical one, then demonstrates it on a real property. This is as much a statement of editorial identity as a single video. — Source: channel-analyst (Speeed round 2) — the anti-hype positioning finding, applied. Platform(s): YouTube. Asset fit: strong — directly leverages editorial credibility as the differentiator it's meant to be. Register: comedic/dry, self-aware. Hook: naming the unspoken rule of sponsored hotel content and then breaking it on camera.

Platform(s): XHS. Asset fit: medium — less about hospitality credentials, more about format novelty; worth a small test. Register: comedic. Hook: total commitment to a joke-premise review category nobody asked for.
