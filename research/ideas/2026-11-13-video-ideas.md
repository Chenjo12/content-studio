1. **"A Tech Billionaire Who Owns 98% of a Hawaiian Island Built His Own Wellness-Hotel Brand With His Own Doctor. Four Seasons Just Quietly Took His Name Off the Building."** A concrete, now-primary-sourced case study for the standing production-queue brief **"Are you paying for the brand or the product?"** (creator-original, selected, no batch source) — closing the sourcing gap this exact lead hit in both the 2026-11-11 and 2026-11-12 batches, where it was found only via a partly-paywalled trade analysis and correctly not proposed. Today's search surfaced Four Seasons' own press release and matching trade reprints, which weren't available in those earlier checks. **The mechanism, in three dated acts**: (1) **The purchase.** Larry Ellison bought roughly 98% of the Hawaiian island of Lanai in 2012 for a reported $200–300M (sources vary on the exact figure) from Dole chairman David Murdock ([Yahoo/Business Insider](https://news.yahoo.com/oracle-billionaire-larry-ellison-moved-124100634.html)). The island's historic Lodge at Koele — built 1990, a Four Seasons property since 2005 — closed in 2015 (at one point reportedly used as temporary housing for construction workers renovating Ellison's other Lanai property, Four Seasons Resort Lanai at Manele Bay) ([Travel Weekly](https://www.travelweekly.com/Hawaii-Travel/Four-Seasons-anticipates-closing-Lanai-lodge-for-most-of-2015)). (2) **The brand Ellison built on top of it.** In 2018, Ellison co-founded a wellness company, Sensei, with Dr. David Agus — a doctor he'd met years earlier through their mutual friend Steve Jobs, for whom Agus had been personal physician ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/03/19/billionaire-larry-ellison-has-a-new-consumer-wellness-company-called-sensei)). After a reported $75M renovation (design: Todd-Avery Lenahan/TAL Studio), the property reopened November 1, 2019 under a hybrid name — "Sensei Lanai, A Four Seasons Resort" — with Sensei operating the wellness programming and Four Seasons running the hotel underneath it, including a Nobu Matsuhisa restaurant ([Hotel Online](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/newly-refurbished-oceanfront-resort-reopens-on-lanai), [Travel Weekly](https://www.travelweekly.com/Hawaii-Travel/Insights/Four-Seasons-Hotel-Lanai-at-Koele-A-Sensei-Retreat-opens-November-1)). (3) **The quiet fold-back, dated this week.** Per Four Seasons' own November 6, 2026 press release, Four Seasons will "resume full management" of the property effective December 16, 2026, renaming it **Four Seasons Resort Lanai at Koele** — dropping "Sensei" from the name entirely. Sensei is stepping back to focus on standalone retreats elsewhere (e.g., Sensei Porcupine Creek in Rancho Mirage, CA), and the restaurant is being renamed "Koele by Nobu." Four Seasons' release frames this as the natural evolution of "an eight-year partnership with Sensei" ([Hospitality Net reprint](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4135331/four-seasons-resorts-lanai-to-resume-full-management-of-sensei-lanai-a-four-seasons-resort), [Hotel News Resource](https://www.hotelnewsresource.com/article143662.html), [WebWire](https://www.webwire.com/ViewPressRel.asp?aId=363857)). **Why this is the pitch, not just an openings/closings brief**: this is a direct, concrete instance of "who is actually running the hotel you're paying for" — a billionaire owner used his own private island to launch a personal wellness-philosophy brand, co-branded it onto a Four Seasons for seven years, and now the house brand has absorbed it back without fanfare, closing the loop so quietly that most coverage this week is describing it as routine "news," not as what it actually is: Four Seasons re-asserting that the hotel brand people actually trust was always the stronger asset than the founder's personal wellness project layered on top of it. **Honest limitations, stated rather than papered over**: the "eight-year partnership" language in Four Seasons' own release doesn't cleanly match the resort's November 2019 reopening date (that's seven years, not eight, to the December 2026 effective date) — likely counting from Sensei's 2018 founding rather than the property's reopening; worth resolving which Four Seasons means before stating a specific duration on camera. The $200M vs. $300M purchase-price discrepancy and the exact 2015 closure date (January vs. June, depending on source) are both unresolved across secondary sources and should be checked against a primary Ellison/Pulama Lanai or Four Seasons statement if cited as a specific figure. No source found this round gives a stated business reason for the change (cost, guest confusion over the hybrid brand name, Sensei's own strategic pivot to standalone retreats) — the piece should present the fold-back as what Four Seasons announced, not speculate on Ellison's or Sensei's internal reasoning without separate confirmation.
