What makes this worth a real insider read rather than a press-release recap: Wynn Palace's Q2 2026 operating revenue hit US$653.4M (up 21.1% YoY), and the actual growth driver wasn't headcount or occupancy — it was the mass-market table games win percentage surging from 22.3% to 29.7% ([IAG](https://asgam.com/2026/08/05/high-win-rates-at-wynn-palace-drive-wynn-macau-ltd-to-13-7-year-on-year-increase-in-2q26-operating-revenues-to-us1-billion/), [AGB](https://agbrief.com/news/macau/05/08/2026/wynns-macau-operating-revenue-tops-1b-in-2q26-led-by-wynn-palace/)). 4. **"The Hotel Built Because No One Would Let Women Check In Alone." — Hotel Figueroa, Los Angeles, 1926.** Opened by the YWCA on August 14, 1926 as a safe haven for solo female travelers, who at the time were barred from checking into most hotels without a male chaperone. The $1.25M raised to build it was, per the LA Times' own 1926 report, "the largest individual financial transaction ever undertaken by a body of women in the United States" — 13 stories, 409 rooms, and its first managing director, Maude N. Bouldin, flew herself cross-country from the East Coast to take the job, becoming the country's first female hotel manager. Men were allowed only on two designated floors ([Islands](https://www.islands.com/1879429/hotel-figueroa-one-only-businesses-america-created-by-for-women-chic-los-angeles/), [LAist](https://laist.com/news/entertainment/how-the-hotel-figueroa-went-from-ywca-haven-to-hipster-hangout), [CNN Travel](https://www.cnn.com/travel/article/hotel-figueroa-women-los-angeles/index.html)). Timely note: the hotel is mid-centennial-celebration right now (dedicated August 14, 1926 — its 100th anniversary was days before this batch). Source: today's pulse-check. Platform(s): YouTube (video essay — this is a narrative that needs the room a short can't give). Asset fit: weak on named-brand tie (an honest editorial-coherence stretch, same category as the Ritz Paris and Manila Hotel entries already in the queue) but strong on real hospitality-industry history a generalist channel wouldn't dig into — the first female hotel manager in the country is a genuinely under-told detail. Register: **history — gripping-dramatic register**, though notably an underdog/triumph story rather than a tragedy — still needs the same factual-rigor bar as the project's disaster-history entries, since it involves real named people (Maude Bouldin) and a specific, checkable dollar figure. Hook: a hotel that exists purely because the rest of the industry decided women traveling alone weren't welcome — so the YWCA built one, ran it themselves, and let men in only if they behaved on the two floors they were allowed.
