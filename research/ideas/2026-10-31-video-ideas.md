## Ideas

1. **"The Free Newspaper Under Your Hotel Door Wasn't a Guest Amenity — It Was Secretly Propping Up a National Newspaper's Circulation Numbers. Then Hotels Quietly Killed It."** The mechanism, verified across multiple sources: Marriott was the first major hotel company to strike an automatic-delivery deal with Gannett to put *USA Today* under every guest's door across its U.S. portfolio. Hotels bought the papers in bulk at a discount and passed the cost through in the room rate — which meant every one of those copies legally counted as **paid circulation** under the industry's own audit rules, even though the "reader" never chose to subscribe and may never have opened it. By 2009, hotel-room copies accounted for **more than half** of *USA Today*'s reported 2.3-million daily circulation. Then guest demand collapsed — Marriott found guest preference for in-room delivery had fallen roughly 25% — and in April 2009 it switched its 2,600+ U.S. hotels from under-the-door delivery to lobby pickup only. Gannett's own estimate: the change alone cost *USA Today* about **50,000 copies a day**. Disney's deluxe resorts followed, ending door delivery in January 2012. ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/news/how-business-travelers-contributed-usa-todays-decline), [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/end-of-era-marriott-pulls-back-on-guest-newspapers), [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/104046/marriott-hotels-ends-automatic-free-newspapers.html), [Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4040877.html)). **The editorial read, not a trivia list**: this isn't really a hotel-amenity story, it's a media-economics story that happens to run through a hotel-room door — a guest amenity was quietly doing double duty as an audited circulation number, and when the hotel industry's own guest-preference data shifted, a national newspaper's "readership" shrank by tens of thousands overnight without losing a single actual subscriber. That's the kind of mechanism a generalist travel channel has no reason to go looking for, and it's a genuine instance of the production queue's established taste for insider-mechanism reveals ("how something in the industry actually works, who checks it, who pays for it") rather than another timeline-of-an-object piece. **Format note, stated honestly because the Rise-of-X queue is already long**: this is structurally closer to "The Rise and Fall of the Tiny Hotel Shampoo Bottle" (a mundane-object piece with a real ending) than to the open-ended Rise-of-X catalog — it earns its place not by being object #19 but because the actual story is about a media-industry audit mechanism, not the newspaper itself. If the creator feels the format queue (now 18 prior Rise-of-X/Rise-and-Fall entries across the batch history) has run its course, this is also easily told as a straight insider-mechanism video without the "Rise of" framing at all. Source: experimental — surfaced via today's historical-mechanism search, not from a live 2026 news hook (the practice's collapse is 2009–2012; no evidence found of a current 2026 angle, and none is claimed). Platform(s): YouTube (the format's proven home), Bilibili (same media/circulation-economics knowledge-lane fit as the Forbes Five-Star Inspector and tipping-history pieces already in the queue), XHS as a tighter cut. Hook: your hotel's free newspaper was never really a gift to you — it was a number someone else was counting, and the day hotels stopped caring, that number fell off a cliff.
