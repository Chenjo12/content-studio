1. **"The Most Expensive 100-Room Hotel Ever Built Went Bankrupt in 18 Months. It's Now One of Florida's Most Famous Resorts."** Fresh find via a Garden & Gun centennial roundup ("Five Storied Hotels Celebrating a Century in 2026"), which surfaced a property whose real history is far bigger than the anniversary blurb: the **Cloister Inn** opened in Boca Raton on **February 6, 1926**, designed by architect Addison Mizner — vaulted ceilings, 14-karat gold-leaf columns, and a reputation (per the Boca Raton Historical Society) as the most expensive 100-room hotel ever built. Mizner had raised the money through his own Mizner Development Corp. at the absolute peak of the Florida land boom, planning a full resort town on 1,600 acres around it. The insider mechanism underneath the glamour: a land boom is a confidence trick that needs speculative buyers to keep paying more than the last buyer, and the September 1926 hurricane popped that confidence within months of the hotel's grand opening. By spring 1927 creditors were suing for their money back; by July 1927 Mizner Development Corp. was bankrupt, along with almost every other developer in South Florida. A Philadelphia utilities magnate, Clarence Geist, picked up the entire project's assets for **$72,000 in November 1927** — a rounding error against what Mizner had spent building the Cloister Inn alone — renamed it the Boca Raton Club, and it eventually became today's **The Boca Raton** (independently branded since 2021, after Waldorf Astoria's management departed; no Bvlgari/Four Seasons/Capella/W/Wynn tie). This is the same "what looks like architecture is actually a financial instrument" read the creator has applied to brand-strategy pieces (Bvlgari-vs-LV, Ritz-Carlton Reserve Shanghai), turned instead on a single building's literal balance sheet — a hotel that's spent a century quietly rebranding a bankruptcy as a legacy. — Source: today's pulse-check ([Garden & Gun](https://gardenandgun.com/southern-century-old-hotels), [Boca Raton Historical Society](https://bocahistory.org/addison-mizner/), [Palm Beach County History Online](https://education.pbchistory.org/land-boom-bust/the-great-architects-mizner-in-boca-raton/), [The Boca Raton — "What's In A Name?"](https://www.thebocaraton.com/blog/whats-in-a-name/), [Wikipedia — The Boca Raton Resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boca_Raton_Resort)).
