Then the Hotel Found Out Ghosts Are a Better Business Than Rooms."** A genuinely new candidate, confirmed via grep as untouched across all 74 prior batches and `research/production-queue.csv` (no hits for "Stanley Hotel," "Shining," "Estes Park," or "Overlook" as an actual subject — the only prior grep hits were unrelated incidental matches on the word "overlooking"). Halloween-timely (one week out) without leaning on the already-declined "most haunted hotels" ghost-legend category (correctly set aside in 2026-10-09, 2026-10-14, and 2026-10-22 for lacking documented history) — the differentiator here is that the core story isn't a ghost claim at all, it's two separately well-documented facts and the business mechanism connecting them. **Fact one, documented, not legend**: Stephen King and his wife Tabitha stayed at Colorado's Stanley Hotel in Estes Park (sources split 1973 vs. late September 1974, per King's own site) when the hotel was closing for the season and running on a skeleton crew — they were reportedly the only paying guests in the building. A dream about his young son being chased down the hotel's long, empty corridors became the seed of *The Shining* ([Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/literature/books/real-hotel-that-inspired-the-shining)). **Fact two, also documented**: the hotel's "haunted" reputation — including stories about its own founder, F.O. Stanley, appearing to guests — predates King's visit; he inherited a legend, he didn't invent one ([Denver Gazette](https://www.denvergazette.com/2019/10/01/the-spooky-story-behind-colorados-haunted-stanley-hotel-d32dd765-7d1a-5775-a886-2d1562f51dc8)). **The actual insider-mechanism payoff — the hospitality-business story underneath the spooky one**: the Stanley now runs nightly ghost tours and paranormal-investigation tours as a real, significant revenue line, especially around Halloween; it holds a sold-out annual "Shining Ball" event (800+ combined tickets per one hotel-trade account of its October signature events); it prices a "Spirited Room" category — including a Stephen King Suite — at a premium specifically because of the reputation; and it built a dedicated "Stanley Film Center" to keep drawing horror-tourism traffic ([Hotel Business archive](https://archive.hotelbusiness.com/haunted-historic-hotels-drive-occupancy-in-october/), [KVIA/CNN Style](https://kvia.com/entertainment/cnn-style/2024/10/29/hotels-are-leaning-into-their-spookier-sides-to-sell-rooms-with-a-boo/)). In 2023 the hotel sold for a reported $475 million to an Arizona nonprofit tasked with operating it in perpetuity specifically as a cultural resource ([Cowboy State Daily](https://cowboystatedaily.com/2023/12/19/made-famous-in-the-shining-historic-stanley-hotel-selling-to-nonprofit/)) — a liability (a nearly-empty, hard-to-fill off-season property) became, decades later, the entire reason the building is worth half a billion dollars. **Honest limitation, stated rather than papered over**: specific ghost-tour and Shining Ball revenue figures aren't independently audited — they trace to hotel-trade press and a GM quote, not financial disclosures, and `story-researcher` should frame them as hotel-reported rather than verified; the exact year of King's stay also varies by source (1973 vs. 1974) and should be pinned down against a primary King-authored source before scripting. The dream/insomnia origin detail is the commonly told version, not independently corroborated beyond King's own retellings. Source: today's pulse-check. Platform(s): YouTube (video essay — the two-fact structure needs room to land the turn from "ghost story" to "business case study"), XHS (shorter cut leading with the empty-hotel-to-$475M-sale arc), Bilibili (same insider-mechanism/knowledge lane as the Forbes Inspector and AHLA-cost pieces already in the queue). Asset fit: moderate-strong — no named-collaboration-brand tie, but this is exactly the editorial-insider reframe CLAUDE.md calls for: reading a famous pop-culture story through hospitality-operations literacy (off-season skeleton-crew staffing, how a reputation gets priced into a room category) rather than retelling it as a travel-blogger ghost-story list. Register: **comedic-dry/ironic**, not gripping — there's no real tragedy here (this is explicitly not the "real victims, real disaster" hotel-history category), and the honest, slightly absurd irony — a hotel got rich off a reputation that was mostly invented by a bored writer with nowhere else to be — is the hook, not a scare. Hook: the most famous haunted hotel in America isn't run by ghosts, it's run by a GM who knows exactly how much a ghost tour sells for in October.
