# Voice notes

Distilled from the creator's own reference posts: a best-performing YouTube video (why they speak English on camera, framed around language/identity/reach — `references/youtube/`) and an XHS post about Sydney's lifestyle vibe (`references/xhs/sydney-lifestyle.md`).

**Self-described inspiration:** Casey Neistat, Peter McKinnon — "straightforward and genuine."

## Traits shared across platforms
- Direct-to-camera. Talking *to* the viewer, not narrating *at* them.
- Short, punchy lines mixed with longer reflective ones — not uniform sentence length. Spoken cadence, not written-essay cadence.
- Real personal anecdotes as the backbone of any abstract point, not just illustration after the fact. Example from the reference video: moving from Beijing to Xiamen partly over accent-based social friction; a disillusioning first trip to Paris that only resolved after learning French.
- Rhetorical questions aimed at the viewer ("Have you ever noticed...", "Can you believe that?").
- Genuine, non-performative code-switching between languages mid-script when it actually serves the point (e.g. slipping into Chinese or French mid-thought) — never code-switching just to show off.
- Grounds abstract ideas (what language *is*, cross-cultural understanding, East/West misunderstanding) in concrete, specific personal stories rather than staying theoretical or generic.
- Comfortable naming real reactions/opinions (disliking Paris at first, finding Parisians rude) rather than staying diplomatically vague — specificity over safety.

**Anti-patterns to avoid:** generic "travel blogger" narration, uniform sentence rhythm, illustrating points with hypotheticals instead of real personal experience, abstract claims left unsupported by a concrete story.

## XHS-specific traits (from the Sydney post)

- Opens with a comparison frame across cities/places before narrowing to the actual subject ("Every city has its own distinct vibe... And in Sydney you've got the sporty and rich vibe") — sets up the destination's identity before describing it.
- Anaphora/repetition as a structural device ("You've got the X vibe in Y" repeated across several cities) to build rhythm before the payoff line.
- Concrete practical details woven directly into the enthusiasm, not separated into a dry "info" section — exact price (10 AUD), exact activities named in a list, specific food items named rather than "great brunch."
- Builds toward a checklist-style summary mid-post ("So you've got the view, the weather, the nature, the facilities...") that recaps what's been said before adding the next beat.
- Closes with a direct, personal, opinion-stated recommendation ("If you want to live healthy and happy, Sydney is the place to be in my opinion") rather than a generic call-to-action.
- Short interjections used for emphasis ("Boom!", "OH and of course") — casual, spoken-aloud energy even in written/caption form.
